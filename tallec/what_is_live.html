What changed

The main changes since the last note.

now live
A new scale: the peer score

A rating of 50 is now the median of the players doing the same job in the same competition and season. It is not a percentile, and an NRL 60 is not a Super League 60; the Translation page moves a rating between competitions.

Every rating, ladder step or translation figure you received before this note is on the old scale. Please do not reuse any of them. That includes the two earlier documents, Reading the Numbers and Four Leagues, One Scale, which now carry a notice saying so.

now live
Every direction between the four competitions

The translation and arrival models are fitted and tested on moves in every direction between the NRL, Super League, the NSW Cup and the Queensland Cup that has enough moves behind it, and the Translation page offers each of those pairs. Moves into Super League are reported first below, because that is where Leeds recruits.

now live
Middles and Edge, and your position sheet

Props and locks are rated together as Middles and the second row on its own as Edge, as Leeds asked. The Position page shows the volume and rate metrics from your position sheet, and says for each competition and season which ones the data cannot support.

fixed
The app is back

All six pages render for all four competitions, checked automatically before this deployment on exactly the files the live app now reads.

now live
Earlier entrants beside every forecast

Where there are enough of them, the translation page now shows earlier players who entered the same competition from the same one with a similar rating: how many, their median, the middle half, and the list, one case per player. Only rated entrants are included, players with three or more matches in the new competition, so it does not measure signing success or show signings who played less. The rule that picks them is fixed in advance, and the card says when there are too few. It sits above the single-number forecast, which is now shown as a reference.

What the numbers say now

Each season from 2023 to 2025 was forecast from what was known before it and scored against what the players actually did, on the 0–100 scale. All of it is exploratory until a season that played no part in it confirms it.

On 90 moves into Super League we found no clear accuracy advantage for the conditional model over a straight line fitted to earlier moves. Both missed a player's rating by about 18 points on average (17.98 for the model, 17.72 for the line; the difference has a 95% range of −1.56 to +1.06). That does not show the two are equally good. In 38 of the 90 forecasts the direction had fewer than 25 earlier moves at the time, so the line used was the one fitted on all directions; the app now follows the same rule. The app's own Queensland Cup line now rests on 32 earlier moves, but that direction never reached 25 during the test, so its own line has not been tested.

Other directions

In exploratory comparisons across the nine directions with enough moves, not adjusted for making nine of them, two favour the model: NRL into the Queensland Cup, by 1.96 points (95% range +0.75 to +3.29), and NSW Cup into the Queensland Cup, by 1.47 points (+0.58 to +2.42). Three favour the line: NRL into the NSW Cup, Queensland Cup into the NSW Cup, and Queensland Cup into Super League. The other four cannot be told apart.

How noisy the target is

A season's rating is an average over a limited number of matches, so it would come out differently from another sample of the same player's matches. Under a simplified repeat-measurement model the target rating varies by about 10 points for these players. That is a sensitivity figure under stated assumptions, not a floor that no forecast can beat, and the rest of the 18-point error cannot be found by subtracting it. Among players with 16 or more Super League matches, whose ratings are steadier, the model and the line still show no clear difference. For reference, a straight line from a Super League player's own previous season, for players who stayed, also misses by about 16 points.

The candidate carried forward

The 2026 test, fixed in advance

One comparison decides it: the app's line against the same line with the indicator, on 2026 entrants into Super League from the NRL, the NSW Cup and the Queensland Cup, scored on 2026 alone. It counts as a positive exploratory signal only if the indicator is at least one rating point closer on average and the 95% range stays above zero. With fewer than 25 entrants, or fewer than five on either side of the indicator, the result is undecided. Everything else is reported as description only. A confirmation would need 2027, under its own specification fixed before any 2027 result is seen.

How to read the rest of this section:

  • Starts for NRL players are rebuilt from interchange records, because that export has no match-sheet positions. Where the rule decides, it agrees with the match sheets of the other seasons 99.98% of the time, and 84% on the rest. It has not been checked on NRL seasons after 2020.
  • Role was measured from who played for the new club, not from what the club planned.

What follows from this

  • The app uses the straight line for moves into Super League as a provisional default, one per source competition, falling back to the line over all directions where a direction has fewer than 25 earlier moves. Whether it is better or worse than the model remains uncertain. From the NRL, for example, the forecast is 0.37 × the NRL rating + 43.
  • Earlier entrants come first on the translation page, which shows how widely real outcomes have spread for players like the one being considered.
  • Dated squad and intended-role information is the most promising next test. We have not established that it is necessary or sufficient to improve the translation.
  • If 2026 supplies about 30 comparable entrants into Super League, adding them to the historical sample could narrow its sampling uncertainty by roughly 13% under similar conditions. That would not be an independent confirmation, which is why the 2026 test is scored on 2026 alone.

Who arrives at all

A separate model asks the step before: of all the players at a given level, which ones turn up in another competition the next season. In exploratory, unadjusted comparisons against a fixed ranking by minutes played, four of twelve directions favour the model. Pooled over 2023–25 and the three source competitions, for moves into Super League it improves on that ranking by +0.168 AUC (95% range +0.093 to +0.247), and its top tenth contains 28 of the 90 players who arrived and were rated. Its usefulness for a single season's shortlist has not been established, and without contract or registration data it cannot tell a signing that worked from one that did not.

Does the player layer help the match model

A retrospective test using the players who actually took part, and their actual minutes, improved an ELO-plus-home baseline by 0.24 points of margin error in the NRL (95% range +0.05 to +0.44, 752 fixtures) and by 1.13 in Super League (+0.62 to +1.63, 497 fixtures). Gains using only what is known before kick-off, the named team and expected minutes, have not been tested yet.

Where that leaves it

Good enough for an internal or beta demo, with the numbers read as described above. Not yet a validated recruitment ranking, and I would not present it to Leeds as one.

What I need from you

In order of how much each would change.

  1. The 2026 season NSW Cup and Queensland Cup 2026, and the NRL 2026 export with the position joined row by row, the way the Super League master came. As you decided, everything is fitted only through 2025, so 2026 becomes the first test of what the ratings and the translation projected.

    Because the method was developed while part of 2026 was already in the data, 2026 will serve as an exploratory test and 2027 as the clean confirmatory one. On arrival the position column gets the checks every position file gets: about 46 minutes for props and 78 for wingers, a roughly thirty-fold spread in passes per minute, about 1.5 positions per player, and no more position values than rows.

  2. Named teams, Super League squads and signing dates We already have every match's actual line-up. What is missing is what was known beforehand: the team named before each round, which the match model needs during the week; each Super League club's squad and squad numbers at the start of the season; and the date each signing was registered. Squad numbers are only a rough guide to the role a club planned, so if a club can give a simple role band for its signings at the time of signing (starter, rotation, depth), that would be better still.
  3. Super League set restarts The column is empty on every Super League row, while all three Australian competitions have it. Six-again is played in Super League, so this is a gap in the extract. Without it, discipline cannot be compared between a Super League forward and an NRL one. Worth asking Stats Perform for.
  4. Two columns from your sheet that the extract does not have Try Assist - Kick and Kick - Forced Dropout. The feed has try assists and tries from kicks, and the dropout taken rather than the kick that forced it, which are not the same things. A fuller export may carry both.
  5. Middles: hit-up metres or line-break assists? Folding locks into Middles meant choosing between the prop's hit-up metres per run and the lock's line-break assists per receipt for one slot. It currently uses hit-up metres. Please confirm, and confirm that "Edge" is the name you want for the second row.
  6. Three scope points to agree Form is calculated over the last five matches; the scope asks for three and five. Divergence is shown as the difference between Form and Class on the underlying scale; the scope asks for a percentage, which is not meaningful on a peer score because the scale has no true zero. Separate ratings for each position a player covers are not built yet, so a player who covers two positions is rated against his most common one.
  7. The metric dictionary All 341 rows are still marked for review. If your position sheet has replaced it, say so and I will retire it.
  8. One row to confirm The Ben Talty reassignment, done as you instructed, produces a round-20 appearance for the Capras, while every other Queensland Cup 2025 row for the player is Burleigh Bears. Is that what you intended?
