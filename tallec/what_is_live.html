What changed

The main changes since the last note.

now live
A new scale: the peer score

A rating of 50 is now the median of the players doing the same job in the same competition and season. It is not a percentile, and an NRL 60 is not a Super League 60; the Translation page moves a rating between competitions.

Every rating, ladder step or translation figure you received before this note is on the old scale. Please do not reuse any of them. That includes the two earlier documents, Reading the Numbers and Four Leagues, One Scale, which now carry a notice saying so.

now live
Every direction between the four competitions

The translation and arrival models are fitted and tested on moves in every direction between the NRL, Super League, the NSW Cup and the Queensland Cup that has enough moves behind it, and the Translation page offers each of those pairs. Moves into Super League are reported first below, because that is where Leeds recruits.

now live
Middles and Edge, and your position sheet

Props and locks are rated together as Middles and the second row on its own as Edge, as Leeds asked. The Position page shows the volume and rate metrics from your position sheet, and says for each competition and season which ones the data cannot support.

What the numbers say now

Each season from 2023 to 2025 was forecast from what was known before it and scored against what the players actually did, on the 0–100 scale.

For the 90 players who moved into Super League, the translation model is no more accurate than a straight line fitted to earlier moves into Super League: both miss a player's rating by about 18 points on average (17.98 for the model, 17.72 for the line; the difference has a 95% range of −1.56 to +1.06).

Where the model adds something

Of the nine directions with enough moves to be scored on their own, the model is clearly closer than the straight line in two: NRL into the Queensland Cup, by 1.96 points (95% range +0.75 to +3.29), and NSW Cup into the Queensland Cup, by 1.47 points (+0.58 to +2.42). In three it is clearly further off than the line: NRL into the NSW Cup, Queensland Cup into the NSW Cup, and Queensland Cup into Super League. In the other four the two cannot be told apart.

How much of that error can be removed at all

A season's rating is itself measured with noise: it is an average over a limited number of matches. Simulating that noise through the rating formula puts a floor of about 10 points under any forecast. Even a forecaster who knew each player's true level would miss by that much on average, so roughly 8 of the 18 points are real differences between what a player's record suggested and what he then did.

The verdict does not depend on that noise. Scored only on players with 16 or more Super League matches, or with the noise removed mathematically, the model and the line remain indistinguishable. For comparison, Super League players who stayed are forecast from their own previous Super League season at about 16 points, so even a player's own record in the league leaves most of those 8 points unexplained: what changes between two seasons is not in the ratings.

None of them clearly improves the forecast. The closest is the trend for moves into Super League, about half a point with a range that only just reaches zero.

How to read this, and where it stops:

  • Twenty comparisons were made, so one or two will look clear by chance. The three results whose whole range is negative are the correction fitting noise in the earlier moves, not a real effect.
  • Role is measured indirectly, from who played for the new club. Who the club actually planned to play is not in the data.
  • * Minutes freed up uses the season being forecast to see who left, so it would not be fully known at signing, and an injured incumbent looks like a departure.
  • xLadder figures exist only for the NRL and Super League, and only from 2022, which covers about a third to a half of the moves.
  • Starts for NRL players are rebuilt from interchange records, because that export has no match-sheet positions. The rule is exact wherever it decides, about three rows in four, and right 84% of the time on the rest.
  • With 90 moves into Super League, an improvement smaller than about a point cannot be shown either way.

What follows from this

  • The app now forecasts moves into Super League with the straight line, one per source competition. It is as accurate as the model and is explained by two numbers: from the NRL, for example, the forecast is 0.37 × the NRL rating + 43.
  • A better translation needs new information, above all the role a player is expected to have at his new club. The versions of it that can be built from appearances did not capture it.
  • The 2026 season adds roughly one more year of moves into Super League, about 30, which narrows the uncertainty only moderately.

Who arrives at all

A separate model asks the step before: of all the players at a given level, which ones turn up in another competition the next season. Across twelve directions it beats minutes played alone clearly in four. Into Super League it does so by +0.168 AUC (95% range +0.093 to +0.247), and the top tenth of its list holds 31% of the players who actually arrived. Use it to order a shortlist, not to put a probability next to a name: it ranks arrivals, and without contract or registration data it cannot tell a signing that worked from one that did not.

Does the player layer help the match model

Over fixtures the model had not seen, adding the player layer cuts margin error by 0.24 points in the NRL (95% range +0.05 to +0.44, 752 fixtures) and by 1.13 points in Super League (+0.62 to +1.63, 497 fixtures). Both ranges exclude zero. Both use the line-up that actually played, which is only known afterwards, so they are an upper bound until team lists exist.

Where that leaves it

Good enough for an internal or beta demo, with the numbers read as described above. Not yet a validated recruitment ranking, and I would not present it to Leeds as one.

What I need from you

In order of how much each would change.

  1. The 2026 season NSW Cup and Queensland Cup 2026, and the NRL 2026 export with the position joined row by row, the way the Super League master came. As you decided, everything is fitted only through 2025, so 2026 becomes the first test of what the ratings and the translation projected.

    Because the method was developed while part of 2026 was already in the data, 2026 will serve as an exploratory test and 2027 as the clean confirmatory one. On arrival the position column gets the checks every position file gets: about 46 minutes for props and 78 for wingers, a roughly thirty-fold spread in passes per minute, about 1.5 positions per player, and no more position values than rows.

  2. Team lists and squads, even typed by hand A list of names per club per round, and each club's squad at the start of the season. It is now the one input that could still improve the translation: the versions of a player's expected role that can be built from appearances added nothing measurable. It is also what turns the match-model result above from an upper bound into something usable during the week.
  3. Super League set restarts The column is empty on every Super League row, while all three Australian competitions have it. Six-again is played in Super League, so this is a gap in the extract. Without it, discipline cannot be compared between a Super League forward and an NRL one. Worth asking Stats Perform for.
  4. Two columns from your sheet that the extract does not have Try Assist - Kick and Kick - Forced Dropout. The feed has try assists and tries from kicks, and the dropout taken rather than the kick that forced it, which are not the same things. A fuller export may carry both.
  5. Middles: hit-up metres or line-break assists? Folding locks into Middles meant choosing between the prop's hit-up metres per run and the lock's line-break assists per receipt for one slot. It currently uses hit-up metres. Please confirm, and confirm that "Edge" is the name you want for the second row.
  6. The metric dictionary All 341 rows are still marked for review. If your position sheet has replaced it, say so and I will retire it.
  7. One row to confirm The Ben Talty reassignment, done as you instructed, produces a round-20 appearance for the Capras, while every other Queensland Cup 2025 row for the player is Burleigh Bears. Is that what you intended?
