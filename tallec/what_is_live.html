What changed since August

Five independent reviews have now been through the method. Each one changed something, and the figures on this page are the ones that survived the fifth.

now live
A new scale: the peer score

Since 22 September a rating of 50 is the median of the players doing the same job in the same competition and season. It is not a percentile, and an NRL 60 is not a Super League 60; the Translation page moves a rating between competitions.

Every rating, ladder step or translation figure from before 22 September is on the old scale. Please do not reuse any of them. That includes the two August documents, Reading the Numbers and Four Leagues, One Scale, which now carry a notice saying so.

now live
Everything is measured on moves into Super League

Leeds recruits into Super League, so the evaluation now targets exactly that: players coming from the NRL, the NSW Cup and the Queensland Cup. For a week in late September the reports treated moves into the NRL as the client's question. The fifth review caught it, and every report has been corrected.

What the numbers say now

The Leeds question on its own: a player is in the NRL, the NSW Cup or the Queensland Cup, so what would he do in Super League?

Each season from 2023 to 2025 was forecast using only what was known before it, then scored against what the players actually did. The figures are points of rating error saved, with 95% intervals; a positive number means the model was closer.

The correction is large and certain: carrying an Australian number into Super League unchanged is the worst thing that can be done with it. The model also beats assuming every arrival is average. What it cannot yet show is that its conditioning on position, age and workload beats a simple straight line, so the claim it supports is a calibrated correction, not a ranking of recruits.

Who arrives at all

A separate model asks the step before: of all the players at a given level, which ones turn up in Super League the next season. It ranks them better than minutes played alone, by +0.168 AUC (interval +0.093 to +0.247), and the top tenth of its list holds 31% of the players who actually arrived. Use it to order a shortlist, not to put a probability next to a name: it ranks arrivals, and without contract or registration data it cannot tell a signing that worked from one that did not.

Does the player layer help the match model

Re-run today on the current database. Over fixtures the model had not seen, adding the player layer cuts margin error by 0.24 points in the NRL (interval +0.05 to +0.44, 752 fixtures) and by 1.13 points in Super League (+0.62 to +1.63, 497 fixtures). Both are real. Both use the line-up that actually played, which is only known afterwards, so they are an upper bound until team lists exist.

Where that leaves it

Good enough for an internal or beta demo, with the numbers read as described above. Not yet a validated recruitment ranking, and I would not present it to Leeds as one.

What I need from you

In order of how much each would change.

  1. The 2026 season NSW Cup and Queensland Cup 2026, and the NRL 2026 export with the position joined row by row, the way the Super League master came. As you decided in August, everything is fitted only through 2025, so 2026 becomes the first test of what the ratings and the translation projected.

    One change to that plan, from the fifth review: the method has been revised several times while 2026 was partly visible, so I will treat 2026 as an exploratory test and keep 2027 as the clean confirmatory one. On arrival the position column gets the same checks the first Super League file failed: about 46 minutes for props and 78 for wingers, a roughly thirty-fold spread in passes per minute, about 1.5 positions per player, and no more position values than rows.

  2. Super League set restarts The column is empty on every Super League row, while all three Australian competitions have it. Six-again is played in Super League, so this is a gap in the extract. Without it, discipline cannot be compared between a Super League forward and an NRL one. Worth asking Stats Perform for.
  3. Two columns from your sheet that the extract does not have Try Assist - Kick and Kick - Forced Dropout. The feed has try assists and tries from kicks, and the dropout taken rather than the kick that forced it, which are not the same things. A fuller export may carry both.
  4. Middles: hit-up metres or line-break assists? Folding locks into Middles meant choosing between the prop's hit-up metres per run and the lock's line-break assists per receipt for one slot. It currently uses hit-up metres. Please confirm, and confirm that "Edge" is the name you want for the second row.
  5. Team lists, even typed by hand A list of names per club per round. It is what turns the match-model result above from an upper bound into something usable during the week.
  6. The metric dictionary All 341 rows are still marked for review. If your position sheet has replaced it, say so and I will retire it.
  7. One row to confirm The Ben Talty reassignment, done as you instructed, produces a round-20 appearance for the Capras, while every other Queensland Cup 2025 row for the player is Burleigh Bears. Is that what you intended?
